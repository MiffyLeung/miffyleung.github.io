01 / THE EXPERIENCE

Practise by playing, not by opening another lesson.

I made No Rush Be Quick as part of CantoMore. The public project introduces Jyutping typing through a browser-based game. CantoMore’s launch description places the experience in Hong Kong transit scenes, with countdown challenges and local chance events.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

One public product within a wider initiative.

I built this web experience within CantoMore. Across the initiative, I lead curriculum design and contribute to early-stage product planning, translating learner needs into activities, game concepts and supporting digital tools.

CantoMore’s wider mission supports newly arrived Mainland Chinese students. This particular game’s public introduction also addresses students and professionals interested in Cantonese typing. The audience for one product does not have to be identical to the audience for the whole initiative.

03 / QUESTIONS I BRING TO THE WORK

Fun is a design choice.
Learning is still a question.

Proposed evaluation—not completed research.

ONBOARDING

Can someone reach a first successful input?

Observe whether a newcomer can begin without prior knowledge of Jyutping, and where help becomes necessary.

PACE

Does urgency invite practice or interrupt it?

Compare how first-time and more experienced players respond to countdowns. Speed should not hide confusion.

TRANSFER

What stays with the player after a round?

Look beyond score. Check whether players can recognise or use the same input in a different context.

04 / WHAT COMES NEXT

A released experience.
Not a finished answer.

The game is publicly accessible. Improvements in typing speed, retention and learning still need to be established; none are claimed here.

Next, I would observe the first attempt, the feedback players notice, and the moments that prompt another round. I would use those observations to decide what to simplify or change, rather than treating a completed game as a completed design problem.
